01 · PLATFORM BASICS

CEX vs DEX: what is the difference?

A centralized exchange (CEX) operates an account-based service and generally holds or controls assets during custody. A decentralized exchange (DEX) uses smart contracts and liquidity pools or on-chain order mechanisms to facilitate swaps.

  • CEXs may offer fiat rails, customer support and familiar order books.
  • DEX users generally connect a self-custody wallet and pay blockchain gas.
  • Neither model removes market, operational, counterparty or smart-contract risk.
02 · TRADING COSTS

Maker and taker fees

Maker orders add liquidity to an order book; taker orders execute against existing liquidity. A market order is commonly taker, while a resting limit order may be maker when it executes.

  • Check the exact product: spot, margin and futures can have different rates.
  • VIP tiers, fee tokens, pair groups and regional rules can change the rate.
  • Also consider spreads, slippage, funding, withdrawal charges and network fees.
03 · SELF-CUSTODY

Understanding crypto wallets

A self-custody wallet lets you control the keys or recovery credentials used to authorize transactions. Wallet apps do not make blockchain transactions free or reversible.

  • Never share a seed phrase or private key, including with support agents.
  • Verify network, token contract and recipient address before sending.
  • Test with a small amount and secure recovery material offline.
04 · DEFI

Swap fees, gas and slippage

A token swap can involve a pool fee, protocol or provider fee, network gas and price impact. The quote may change before confirmation, especially in thin or volatile markets.

  • Compare the final received amount, not just the headline fee.
  • Review token approvals and revoke allowances you no longer need.
  • Beware of fake tokens, malicious links and imitation dapps.
05 · PROMOTIONS

How to assess a crypto bonus

A headline reward is not necessarily cash or immediately withdrawable. Promotions may require identity checks, deposits, trading volume, lockups or geographic eligibility.

  • Read the official terms and check the expiry date.
  • Understand minimum deposits, wagering/trading conditions and clawbacks.
  • Do not deposit funds solely to chase a bonus you do not understand.
06 · RISK MANAGEMENT

Risks to understand first

Crypto assets can be highly volatile and may lose substantial or all of their value. Leverage can magnify losses and trigger liquidation; platform failures and regulatory changes can also affect access.

  • Never risk money you cannot afford to lose.
  • Understand liquidation price, funding and collateral rules before using leverage.
  • Check local laws, tax treatment and provider eligibility independently.
