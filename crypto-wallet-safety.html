Crypto wallet safety: protect your keys and transactions.

A wallet is more than an app. It is part of the system that controls access to digital assets. Learn how recovery phrases, addresses, networks and token approvals work—and where common mistakes happen.

AUTOMATET Editorial · Published 25 November 2026 · Educational content, not security or investment advice
The most important rule

Never share your recovery phrase or private key. Anyone who obtains the credentials that control a self-custody wallet may be able to move its assets. Legitimate support staff should not need your recovery phrase to help you.

1. Understand what your wallet controls

A self-custody wallet helps you manage the keys used to authorize blockchain transactions. The wallet application is an interface; the assets are recorded on the relevant blockchain. Losing access to a device does not necessarily mean the assets are lost if you have a valid recovery method, but losing the recovery credentials can make access impossible.

A custodial exchange account works differently: the provider controls the underlying keys and gives you account access under its terms. Understand which model you are using before depositing assets.

2. Protect your recovery phrase and private keys

  • Write the recovery phrase down offline and store it in a private, secure place.
  • Do not photograph it, email it, message it to yourself or store it in an ordinary cloud note.
  • Never enter it into a website or form reached through an unsolicited message.
  • Be cautious of fake support agents, giveaway claims and urgent account-warning messages.
  • Consider a hardware wallet for larger holdings, while learning its setup and backup process first.
A recovery phrase is not a password-reset code. Treat it as the master credential for the wallet it restores.

3. Verify the network and receiving address

Crypto assets can exist on multiple networks, and similar-looking token names may refer to different contracts. Before sending, confirm that the receiving service supports the exact asset and network you selected.

  • Check the full address and network on both the sending and receiving sides.
  • Do not rely only on a contact name, logo, QR-code label or first and last few characters.
  • Beware of address-poisoning attempts that place lookalike addresses in transaction history.
  • For a new destination or a large transfer, consider a small test transaction first and wait for confirmation.
  • Remember that on-chain transfers are usually irreversible once confirmed.

4. Understand token approvals

Some decentralized applications ask a wallet to approve a smart contract to spend a token on your behalf. An approval can remain active after the immediate transaction. The scope may be limited to a specific amount or may allow a larger amount, depending on what you sign.

  • Read the wallet prompt and identify the token and spender contract.
  • Approve only what is needed where the wallet and application allow it.
  • Use the official application address and verify it through trusted project channels.
  • Review old allowances periodically and revoke permissions you no longer need using a trusted tool for the correct network.

Revoking an approval may require a blockchain transaction and network fee. A revocation interface is itself a security-sensitive destination, so verify it before connecting a wallet.

5. Recognize common phishing patterns

  • Messages claiming your wallet will be frozen unless you connect immediately.
  • Fake airdrops, claim pages or support accounts asking for a recovery phrase.
  • Sponsored search results or copied websites using slightly altered domain names.
  • Unexpected wallet pop-ups requesting signatures unrelated to the action you intended.
  • Impersonators asking you to install remote-access software or share your screen.

Bookmark official sites you have independently verified. Do not use links from unsolicited direct messages, comments or emails to access a wallet or exchange account.

6. Read transaction and signature prompts

Not every wallet prompt is a simple transfer. A signature may authorize a message, login, order or token permission. If the purpose or requested permissions are unclear, reject the request and verify it through the project's official documentation. Never sign a prompt just because a website says it is required to receive a reward.

7. A simple wallet security routine

  • Keep wallet software and device operating systems updated from official sources.
  • Use a device lock and strong, unique passwords where supported.
  • Separate everyday wallet activity from long-term storage where practical.
  • Keep recovery material offline and check that your backup process is understood.
  • Verify network, address, token contract and transaction details before confirming.
  • Review connected sites and token approvals periodically.
  • Never disclose private keys, recovery phrases, one-time codes or remote-access credentials.

Frequently asked questions

Can AUTOMATET or a wallet support agent recover my seed phrase?

No. A legitimate service should not ask you to disclose your recovery phrase. Anyone claiming they can validate or synchronize a wallet by collecting it should be treated as suspicious.

Is a hardware wallet completely safe?

No device removes all risk. Hardware wallets can reduce exposure of private keys, but users still need to protect backups, verify addresses and understand the transactions they approve.

Should I approve unlimited token spending?

Only make an approval after understanding the spender and the amount. Where supported, a limited approval can reduce exposure, but it does not eliminate smart-contract or phishing risk.

Can I reverse a crypto transfer sent to the wrong network?

Often there is no simple reversal. Recovery depends on the asset, network, destination and whether you control the receiving address. Confirm compatibility before sending.
